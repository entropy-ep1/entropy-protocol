 IARC concluded that there is “convincing evidence” of the link between processed meat and bowel cancer. The link between red meat and cancer is less certain, but there is “limited evidence” that it may cause bowel cancer. The report, published in the Lancet Oncology journal, suggests that eating 50 grams of processed meat a day (about one sausage or two slices of bacon) increases the risk of bowel cancer by 18%. The IARC also stated that red meat probably causes cancer, but the evidence is not as strong. The findings are based on a review of over 800 studies conducted in the last 20 years. The report says that the classification does not mean that these foods are equally dangerous as asbestos and tobacco. It only means that there is sufficient evidence that these foods are linked to cancer. The IARC has a 10-category classification system, and the groupings do not necessarily mean the same risk. It says that red meat is in the second category of “probably carcinogenic to humans” while processed meat is in the top category, meaning that there is “sufficient evidence” of the cancer link. Processed meat includes any type of meat that has been transformed through salting, curing, fermentation, smoking or other processes to enhance flavor or improve preservation. Examples include bacon, sausages, ham, corned beef, and processed meat products. Processed meat is a group of foods that include meat preserved by smoking, curing or salting, or by the addition of chemical preservatives. The meat may come from pork, beef, poultry or other sources. Red meat includes any meat from dark, red or purplish-red cuts of beef, lamb, pork, game and other mammals.
