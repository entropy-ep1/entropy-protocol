Before the invention of the printing press, books were copied by hand, making them expensive and rare. The printing press, invented by Johannes Gutenberg in the middle of the 15th century, allowed for the mass production of books and other documents. As a result, knowledge became more widely accessible, leading to a significant increase in literacy rates. The printing press played a crucial role in the spread of ideas during the Renaissance and Reformation periods, as it allowed for the rapid dissemination of new thoughts and ideas throughout Europe and beyond. With the ability to produce books quickly and at a lower cost, scholars, philosophers, and scientists could share their work with a wider audience, leading to significant advancements in various fields of study.
